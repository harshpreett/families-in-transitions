PAGE 02 · WORKING WITH FAMILIES

Partnership begins
with listening.

Families are children’s first and continuing teachers. They know their child’s personality, interests, routines, culture and needs. Educators build on that knowledge through respectful, two-way relationships.

SIX WAYS TO CONNECT

Make room for every family

Family engagement is an ongoing exchange. Ask, listen and adapt so families can participate in ways that fit their lives.

01

Build a warm welcome

Learn family members’ names and preferred ways to communicate. Listen with care, respect different family structures and parenting practices, and make the first hello count.

02

Keep communication two-way

Share brief updates about a child’s activities, strengths and progress. Invite families to share what they notice at home. Conversations, notes, email and meetings can all help; protect confidentiality in every format.

03

Invite family contributions

Ask whether a family member would like to share a story, song, language, tradition, skill or interest. Offer different times and ways to join in because schedules and resources differ.

04

Plan with families

Ask about family goals and concerns. Listen to ideas and agree on practical plans together for a child’s learning, routines or additional support.

05

Make participation flexible

There is no single right way to be involved. Offer low-pressure options that respect families’ time, language preferences, comfort and access needs.

06

Connect to community supports

With a family’s agreement, share information about libraries, public health, family resource programs, settlement services and other local supports.

A TWO-WAY RELATIONSHIP

Families bring expertise.

When educators make space for family knowledge, children experience more continuity between home and their early learning setting. The College of Early Childhood Educators describes engagement as communication and collaboration with families.
