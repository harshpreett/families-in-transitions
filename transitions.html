PAGE 03 · CHANGE AND CONTINUITY

When life changes,
care stays close.

Family transitions are changes in relationships, living arrangements or a child’s daily routine. Some are planned; others arrive unexpectedly. Each family’s experience is different.

TRANSITIONS CAN INCLUDE

Many kinds of change

Starting child care or school, moving home, a parent returning to work, separation or divorce, a new baby, illness, migration, or a change in caregiving arrangements may all shift family routines.

WHAT FAMILIES MAY FACE

Challenges can overlap

♡

Emotional stress

Uncertainty, grief, worry or a need for extra reassurance can accompany a major change.

◷

Routine and care

New schedules, transportation needs or difficulty finding suitable child care can affect daily life.

⌂

Practical pressure

Families may face financial strain, language barriers or changes to work and caregiving arrangements.

CHILD CARE ACCESS IN CANADA

Finding a space can be difficult.

Statistics Canada has reported barriers such as a shortage of spaces and affordability challenges. Some parents changed work schedules or delayed returning to work because of child-care difficulties. These pressures can add to the stress of an already busy transition.

HOW EDUCATORS CAN HELP

Offer steadiness, one step at a time

Children may need time to feel comfortable with a new place, caregiver, classroom or routine. Observe each child’s responses rather than assuming every child feels the same way.

01

Prepare with care

Use photographs, stories, visits and simple, age-appropriate explanations to help children know what to expect.

02

Keep familiar rhythms

Make routines predictable where possible and offer a warm, familiar welcome in a new setting.

03

Ask the family

Find out what helps the child feel safe. Respect family knowledge, culture and practices.

04

Agree on next steps

Stay in regular contact and plan practical steps together. With family consent, connect families to supports when needs go beyond the educator’s role.

CONTINUITY BUILDS TRUST
Strong partnerships help children carry a sense of safety between home and their early learning setting.

Educators can remain respectful, inclusive and confidential while avoiding assumptions about a family’s circumstances.
